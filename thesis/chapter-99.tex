\chaptoc{Conclusion}

The main objective of this thesis is to develop software that helps people solve Sudoku variants
by applying strategies from simplest to most complex.
This objective was achieved.
The software supports several types of Sudoku regions --- rectangular, jigsaw, diagonal, and extra regions.
It supports adding one of five variants: Consecutive, Kropki, VX, Comparative, and Sum.
A user can configure a Sudoku task in the UI and then apply multiple solving strategies.
The supported strategies are: Pointing, Naked Candidates, Hidden Candidates, and Generalised X-Wing.
There are two general strategies applicable to all variants: Edge Label and Edge Region.
Using these strategies, the software provides step-by-step explanation of solving the Sudoku variant.

The thesis explains and proves Algorithm~\ref{alg:algorithm} that finds the smallest application of Naked Candidates,
Hidden Candidates, and Generalised X-Wing with time complexity $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ in Chapter~\ref{chap:5}.
This algorithm is important, because the trivial method of looking through all possible combinations
of cells would have time complexity $\mathcal{O}(p(n) \cdot 2^n)$.
This was not part of the original objective, but became significant after the algorithm was implemented.

Future work could extend this thesis by adding more Sudoku variants or by adding more solving strategies,
as explained in Section~\ref{sec:extensibility}.
